## 1. Russian missiles land in Poland

- Two Russian missiles and a suspected jet-powered drone came down in south-eastern Poland on Friday 20 November 2026, during a large Russian attack on Ukraine (Russia fired more than 400 drones and 110 missiles at Ukraine overnight, per President Zelenskyy).
- Polish Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz identified one object as a Kh-101 cruise missile, found near Nowy Lipowiec; the others came down near Rudnik nad Sanem and Molodycz. No one in Poland was hurt and no infrastructure was destroyed.
- Prime Minister Donald Tusk said it appeared to be "an intentional provocation" by Russia; NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte called it a "grave violation" of Polish airspace and was due to visit Warsaw on Saturday.

## 3. Trump wants Ukraine deal by January, FT reports

- The Trump administration told European and Ukrainian officials it wants a peace deal between Ukraine and Russia by early January 2027, before Democrats take control of the US Congress, the Financial Times reported on 20 November, citing four people familiar with the negotiations.
- Envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner resumed talks with Ukrainian and Russian representatives in Abu Dhabi after an eight-month hiatus, and held meetings in Moscow, Dublin and Berlin.

## 4. World court: Myanmar committed genocide

- On Thursday 19 November 2026 the International Court of Justice found, by 9 votes to 6, that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in northern Rakhine State during the 2016–2017 clearance operations, in violation of the Genocide Convention — the first time the court has found a state responsible for genocide.
- The court ordered Myanmar to investigate and prosecute those responsible, provide reparations and compensation, and allow the safe, voluntary and dignified return of displaced Rohingya. Myanmar must report on restitution measures within two years.

## 5. Britain and EU seal food and youth deals

- Britain and the European Union sealed deals at a summit in Brussels on Friday 20 November 2026 on easing food trade, linking emissions trading systems and youth mobility, in a reset of ties. The summit had been postponed from July after the resignation of Prime Minister Andy Burnham's predecessor Keir Starmer.
- The food deal allows cross-border trade in animals, plants and related products without the certificates and controls now required; Britain estimates it could add up to £5.1 billion a year to its economy.
- Each side will allow 100,000 young people a year in to work or study for up to three years. The deals apply from 2028, with the first young people benefiting from youth mobility by 2030. Agreement on university tuition fees was put off for six months.

## 6. Cancer pill shrinks pancreatic tumours in 44% of patients

- Results from the AURORAS-1 Phase 1 trial of Erasca's ERAS-0015, a pan-RAS molecular glue pill, were presented on Friday 20 November 2026 at the 38th EORTC-NCI-AACR Symposium in Barcelona.
- Tumours shrank in 11 of 25 patients (44%) with previously treated RAS-mutant pancreatic cancer who received the 32 mg dose; the response rate was 60% in RAS-mutant lung cancer and 69% in the most common pancreatic cancer mutations (KRAS G12D/G12V).
- The trial has enrolled 148 patients so far; follow-up is about four months, so it is too soon to know how long responses last, and the data are from an early Phase 1 trial, not a peer-reviewed publication.
